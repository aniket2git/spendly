6.2 Grammar Rules That Are Actually Tested

6.2.1 Subject-verb agreement - the highest-yield rule set

RuleCorrect usage
Singular subject takes a singular verbThe boy plays.
Two subjects joined by 'and' take a plural verbRam and Shyam are here.
But if 'and' joins one idea, use singularBread and butter is my breakfast.
Subjects joined by or / nor / either-or / neither-nor: verb agrees with the NEAREST subjectNeither the students nor the teacher was present.
'Each', 'every', 'either', 'neither', 'everyone', 'nobody', 'anyone' are always SINGULAREach of the boys is present.
'One of' + plural noun + singular verbOne of my friends is a doctor.
Collective nouns take singular when acting as one unitThe team is playing well.
Uncountable nouns are singularThe information is useful.
'The number of' takes singular; 'a number of' takes pluralThe number of students is rising. A number of students are absent.
Distance, time, money, weight as one quantity take singularTen kilometres is a long walk. Fifty rupees is not enough.
'Police', 'people', 'cattle', 'gentry' are always PLURALThe police have arrived.
'News', 'mathematics', 'physics', 'economics', 'politics' end in -s but are SINGULARThe news is good. Mathematics is difficult.
'A pair of' takes singularA pair of shoes is missing.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Spot the error: "Each of the students / have submitted / their assignment / on time."

  • (a) Each of the students
  • (b) have submitted <-- CORRECT
  • (c) their assignment
  • (d) on time
Why: "Each" is always singular, so it needs "has submitted", not "have". This is the single most frequently tested rule in the section. Related singular words: each, every, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anyone, nobody, someone, none.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Choose the correct sentence.

  • (a) Neither of the boys were present.
  • (b) Neither of the boys was present. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) Neither of the boys are present.
  • (d) Neither of the boys have present.
Why: "Neither" is singular, so it takes "was". The plural "boys" immediately before the verb is the deliberate distraction - the verb agrees with neither, not with boys.

Q. [EXPECTED] [TRAP] Choose the correct sentence.

  • (a) The number of accidents are increasing.
  • (b) The number of accidents is increasing. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) A number of accidents is increasing.
  • (d) Numbers of accidents is increasing.
Why: "The number of" is a single quantity, hence singular ("is"). But "a number of" means "several" and is plural ("A number of accidents are reported daily"). Learn both together - examiners test the pair.

6.2.2 Tenses

RuleExample
Universal truths and habitual actions take simple presentThe sun rises in the east.
'Since' and 'for' with a period take present perfect continuousHe has been living here since 2010.
After 'if' in a conditional, do not use 'will'If it rains, we will stay home. (NOT "if it will rain")
Two past actions: the earlier takes past perfectThe train had left before I reached.
'Yesterday', 'ago', 'last night' need simple pastHe went yesterday. (NOT "has gone yesterday")
After 'wish' and 'if only', use past subjunctiveI wish I were rich. (NOT "was")
'It is time' takes past tenseIt is time we left.
After 'as if' / 'as though', use pastHe behaves as if he were the boss.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Fill in the blank: "He __ in this city since 2015."

  • (a) lives
  • (b) has been living <-- CORRECT
  • (c) is living
  • (d) lived
Why: "Since" with a starting point requires the present perfect continuous for an action still going on. Remember: since takes a point in time (since Monday), for takes a duration (for three years).

Q. [EXPECTED] [TRAP] Choose the correct sentence.

  • (a) If he will come, I will meet him.
  • (b) If he comes, I will meet him. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) If he came, I will meet him.
  • (d) If he come, I will meet him.
Why: In a first-conditional sentence, the if-clause takes the simple present and the main clause takes "will". Putting "will" in the if-clause is one of the most common errors tested.

6.2.3 Articles

RuleExample
'A' before a consonant SOUNDa book, a university (yoo sound), a one-rupee note (wun sound)
'An' before a vowel SOUNDan apple, an hour (silent h), an MP (em sound), an honest man
'The' with unique thingsthe sun, the moon, the Earth
'The' with rivers, seas, mountain ranges, groups of islandsthe Ganga, the Himalayas
NO 'the' with single mountains, lakes, continents, countries (usually)Mount Everest, Lake Chilka, Asia, India
'The' with superlatives and ordinalsthe best, the first
'The' before an adjective used as a noun for a classthe poor, the rich
No article before languages, meals, gamesHe speaks English. We had lunch. He plays cricket.
'The' with the names of newspapers, ships, holy booksThe Hindu, The Ramayana

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] [TRAP] Fill in the blank: "He is __ honest man."

  • (a) a
  • (b) an <-- CORRECT
  • (c) the
  • (d) no article
Why: "Honest" begins with a silent h, so the sound is a vowel - hence "an". The rule depends on sound, not spelling. Compare "a hotel" (h pronounced) and "an hour" (h silent); also "a university" (yoo) but "an umbrella" (uh).

Q. [EXPECTED] Fill in the blank: "__ Ganga is a sacred river."

  • (a) A
  • (b) An
  • (c) The <-- CORRECT
  • (d) No article
Why: Rivers always take "the". So do seas, oceans, mountain ranges, deserts and groups of islands. Single mountains do not - "Mount Everest", never "the Mount Everest".

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] [TRAP] Fill in the blank: "He is superior __ me in intelligence."

  • (a) than
  • (b) to <-- CORRECT
  • (c) from
  • (d) with
Why: Latin comparatives - superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, posterior, prefer - all take "to", never "than". "He is senior to me", "I prefer tea to coffee".

Q. [EXPECTED] [TRAP] Choose the correct sentence.

  • (a) The committee comprises of ten members.
  • (b) The committee comprises ten members. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) The committee is comprised with ten members.
  • (d) The committee comprise of ten members.
Why: "Comprise" takes no preposition. Use "consists of" or "is composed of", but "comprises" alone. This is a favourite error to plant.

6.2.5 Active and passive voice

*Voice conversion rules*

ACTIVE : Subject + Verb + Object
PASSIVE: Object + form of BE + past participle + by + Subject

   Active : Ram writes a letter.
   Passive: A letter is written by Ram.

TENSE MAPPING
   Simple present     : is / am / are + V3      -> is written
   Present continuous : is / am / are being + V3 -> is being written
   Present perfect    : has / have been + V3     -> has been written
   Simple past        : was / were + V3          -> was written
   Past continuous    : was / were being + V3    -> was being written
   Past perfect       : had been + V3            -> had been written
   Simple future      : will be + V3             -> will be written
   Future perfect     : will have been + V3      -> will have been written
   Modals             : can/may/must be + V3     -> can be written

IMPERATIVE sentences become "Let ..."
   Active : Open the door.
   Passive: Let the door be opened.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Change to passive voice: "The teacher punished the student."

  • (a) The student punished by the teacher.
  • (b) The student was punished by the teacher. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) The student is punished by the teacher.
  • (d) The student has punished by the teacher.
Why: Simple past active becomes was/were + past participle: "was punished". Keeping the tense correct is what the question tests.

Q. [EXPECTED] Change to passive: "Someone has stolen my bag."

  • (a) My bag has stolen.
  • (b) My bag has been stolen. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) My bag is stolen.
  • (d) My bag was stolen.
Why: Present perfect active becomes has/have been + V3. Note that when the subject is vague ("someone"), the "by" phrase is normally dropped.

6.2.6 Direct and indirect speech

*Narration rules*

1. REPORTING VERB in past -> shift the reported tense one step back:
      present -> past ;  past -> past perfect ;  will -> would
      can -> could ;  may -> might ;  must -> had to
   (No shift if the reporting verb is in the present, or if the
    statement is a universal truth.)

2. PRONOUNS change according to the speaker and listener.

3. TIME AND PLACE words shift:
      now -> then            today -> that day
      tomorrow -> the next day    yesterday -> the previous day
      here -> there          this -> that       ago -> before

4. CONNECTOR by sentence type:
      Statement  -> that
      Question (wh-)  -> the same wh-word
      Question (yes/no) -> if / whether
      Command / request -> to / not to  (verb: ordered, requested, advised)
      Exclamation -> that, with the reporting verb 'exclaimed'

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Convert: He said, "I am busy."

  • (a) He said that I am busy.
  • (b) He said that he was busy. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) He says that he is busy.
  • (d) He said that he is busy.
Why: Two changes: the pronoun "I" becomes "he" (referring to the speaker), and the present "am" shifts back to "was" because the reporting verb "said" is past.

Q. [EXPECTED] Convert: She said, "Where is my book?"

  • (a) She asked where is my book.
  • (b) She asked where her book was. <-- CORRECT
  • (c) She said where her book was.
  • (d) She asked where was her book.
Why: For a wh-question, keep the wh-word as the connector, change the reporting verb to "asked", shift the pronoun ("my" to "her"), and convert to statement word order ("her book was", not "was her book").

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] What does 'to beat about the bush' mean?

  • (a) To work hard
  • (b) To avoid the main topic <-- CORRECT
  • (c) To search everywhere
  • (d) To make noise
Why: The idiom means talking around a subject without addressing it directly. Note the trap "to search everywhere" - a literal reading of the words, which is exactly how idiom questions are built.

6.4 Sentence Rearrangement and Comprehension

6.4.1 Rearranging sentences

*THE METHOD*

Step 1: Find the OPENING sentence. It introduces the subject with a
        full noun, not a pronoun, and depends on nothing before it.
Step 2: Find the CLOSING sentence - a conclusion, result or summary.
Step 3: Use LINK WORDS to chain the middle:
           however, therefore, but, moreover, thus, consequently,
           on the other hand, finally
Step 4: Track PRONOUNS. A sentence beginning "He/She/It/They/This"
        must follow the sentence naming that thing.
Step 5: Follow CHRONOLOGY or logical cause-and-effect.
Step 6: Read your final order once, straight through, to check it flows.
TIP: The fastest way into a rearrangement question

Do not try to order all four or five parts at once. Instead, find the one sentence that cannot possibly be first (because it starts with a pronoun or a connector) and the one that must be first. That usually eliminates two or three of the four options immediately.

6.4.2 Reading comprehension

*THE METHOD - questions before passage*

Step 1: Read the QUESTIONS first, quickly. Now you know what to look for.
Step 2: Skim the passage once for its overall subject and tone.
Step 3: Answer the FACTUAL questions first by locating the exact line.
Step 4: Answer inference and tone questions last.
Step 5: For vocabulary-in-context questions, substitute each option back
        into the original sentence and see which preserves the meaning.

GOLDEN RULE: the answer must come from the PASSAGE, never from your own
knowledge of the world. If an option is true in real life but is not
stated or implied in the passage, it is WRONG.
Question typeHow to handle it
Direct / factualLocate the line. The answer is stated
Vocabulary in contextSubstitute each option into the sentence
InferenceMust follow necessarily from the text, not merely be plausible
Main idea / titleShould cover the whole passage, not one paragraph
Tone / attitudeLook at adjectives and adverbs the writer chooses
"The author would agree with"Find explicit support in the text
TIP: The two traps in every comprehension set

Trap 1: the too-broad or too-narrow title. For "best title" questions, reject options that describe only one paragraph, and reject options broader than the passage.

Trap 2: the true-but-irrelevant option. Examiners include statements that are factually correct in the real world but absent from the passage. Your only authority is the passage.

6.5 English Revision Sheet

6.5.1 The twenty rules that carry the marks

*The rules worth memorising verbatim*

1.  Each / every / either / neither / everyone / nobody = SINGULAR.
2.  'One of' + plural noun + SINGULAR verb.
3.  Or / nor / either-or / neither-nor: verb agrees with the NEAREST subject.
4.  'The number of' = singular;  'a number of' = plural.
5.  News, mathematics, physics, economics, politics = SINGULAR.
6.  Police, people, cattle, gentry = PLURAL.
7.  Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, prefer + TO (never 'than').
8.  'Comprise' takes NO preposition.
9.  Article choice depends on SOUND, not spelling (an hour, a university).
10. Rivers, seas, mountain ranges, holy books, newspapers take THE.
11. Single mountains, lakes, continents take NO article.
12. In an if-clause use the simple present, never 'will'.
13. 'Since' + point in time;  'for' + duration.
14. Two past actions: the earlier one takes past perfect (had + V3).
15. After 'wish', 'as if', 'it is time': use the PAST form (I wish I were).
16. Passive = form of BE + past participle (V3) + by.
17. Imperative in passive becomes 'Let ... be ...'.
18. Reporting verb in past shifts the reported tense one step back.
19. Yes/no question in indirect speech takes 'if' or 'whether'.
20. In comprehension, the passage is the ONLY authority.

6.5.2 Habits that produce 17-19 out of 20

  1. Learn ten new words a day, in sentences. Isolated word lists decay; words in context stick. Six weeks of this covers most of what these papers ask.
  2. Do error-spotting daily. It is the most rule-dense question type, so it improves fastest with practice.
  3. When spotting errors, check in a fixed order: subject-verb agreement, then tense, then preposition, then article, then pronoun. A systematic scan catches errors that reading "by feel" misses.
  4. Read the comprehension questions before the passage. It halves the time you spend re-reading.
  5. Never answer comprehension from general knowledge. If it is not in the passage, it is not the answer - regardless of whether it is true.
