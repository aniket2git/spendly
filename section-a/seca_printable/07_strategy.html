Do not start a new book, a new test series or a new current-affairs source. Do not attempt a topic you have never touched - a topic learned in the final week is not retained under exam pressure, and the hours are better spent making certain things certain.

Do not stay up late the night before. This exam is 200 questions in 120 minutes, decided almost entirely by speed of recall. Recall is the first cognitive function to degrade with sleep loss. A rested candidate who knows 80% beats a tired candidate who knows 90%.

8.2 The One-Page Formula Sheet

8.2.1 Mathematics

*Everything you must recall instantly*

PERCENTAGE
   Percentage change = (change / ORIGINAL) x 100
   Increase then decrease by x%  -> net DECREASE of x^2/100 %
   Successive changes a% and b%  -> a + b + ab/100
   A is x% more than B -> B is 100x/(100+x) % less than A

PROFIT AND LOSS
   Profit% on COST price ;  Discount% on MARKED price
   Successive discounts a% and b% -> a + b - ab/100
   Same profit% and loss% at same SP -> net LOSS of x^2/100 %
   Faulty weight gain% = [error / (true - error)] x 100

INTEREST
   SI = PRT/100
   CI: A = P(1 + R/100)^T
   CI - SI for 2 years = P(R/100)^2
   Doubling time at SI = 100/R years

RATIO AND AVERAGE
   Divide X in a:b:c -> Xa/(a+b+c) etc.
   Mean proportional of a and b = sqrt(ab)
   Average of first n naturals = (n+1)/2
   Sum of first n naturals = n(n+1)/2
   Replace one value: new avg = old + (new - old)/n

TIME AND WORK
   A and B together = ab/(a+b) days
   Outlet pipe = NEGATIVE work
   M1 D1 H1 / W1 = M2 D2 H2 / W2

SPEED
   km/hr to m/s: x 5/18        m/s to km/hr: x 18/5
   Equal DISTANCES at x and y -> average = 2xy/(x+y)
   Equal TIMES at x and y     -> average = (x+y)/2
   Train + platform = both lengths ; train + pole = train length only
   Same direction: subtract speeds ; opposite: add speeds
   Boat: speed = (down+up)/2 ; stream = (down-up)/2

MENSURATION
   Square diagonal = a sqrt2 ; Cube diagonal = a sqrt3
   Circle: area pi r^2, circumference 2 pi r
   Equilateral triangle area = (sqrt3/4) a^2
   Cylinder volume = pi r^2 h ; Cone = (1/3) pi r^2 h
   Sphere volume = (4/3) pi r^3 ; surface = 4 pi r^2
   Double the radius -> area x4, volume x8

NUMBERS
   HCF x LCM = product of the two numbers
   Same remainder r -> answer = LCM + r
   Constant (divisor - remainder) = d -> answer = LCM - d
   Divisibility by 11: (odd-position sum) - (even-position sum)

PERCENTAGE-FRACTION (learn cold)
   50%=1/2  33.33%=1/3  25%=1/4  20%=1/5  16.67%=1/6
   14.28%=1/7  12.5%=1/8  11.11%=1/9  10%=1/10  6.25%=1/16

8.2.2 Reasoning

*Everything you must recall instantly*

ALPHABET     EJOTY: E=5 J=10 O=15 T=20 Y=25
             Reverse position = 27 - position

RANKING      Total = top rank + bottom rank - 1
             Between two = difference from same end - 1

DIRECTION    Shortest = sqrt(a^2 + b^2)
             Triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25
             Facing SOUTH reverses left and right

CLOCK        Angle = |30H - 5.5M|  (over 180? subtract from 360)
             Coincide 22 times a day ; right angles 44 times a day

CALENDAR     Ordinary year = 1 odd day ; leap = 2 odd days
             Leap: div by 4, century must be div by 400
             0=Sunday ... 6=Saturday

DICE/CUBE    Opposite faces sum to 7
             n^3 cut: 3 faces=8, 2 faces=12(n-2),
                      1 face=6(n-2)^2, 0 faces=(n-2)^3

MIRROR       Safe letters: A H I M O T U V W X Y
WATER        Safe letters: B C D E H I K O X ; digits 0 1 3 8

SYLLOGISM    All+All=All ; Some+All=Some ; All+Some=nothing
             "Some" reverses ; "All" does NOT reverse

8.2.3 English and Hindi

*The rules that carry the language marks*

ENGLISH
   Each/every/either/neither/everyone/nobody = SINGULAR
   'One of' + plural noun + SINGULAR verb
   Or/nor: verb agrees with the NEAREST subject
   'The number of' singular ; 'a number of' plural
   News, mathematics, physics, economics = SINGULAR
   Police, people, cattle = PLURAL
   Superior/inferior/senior/junior/prefer + TO (never 'than')
   'Comprise' takes NO preposition
   Articles follow SOUND not spelling (an hour, a university)
   No 'will' in an if-clause
   'Since' + point in time ; 'for' + duration
   Earlier of two past actions -> past perfect
   After wish / as if / it is time -> use PAST (I wish I were)
   Passive = BE + past participle + by
   Reporting verb in past -> shift reported tense one step back
   Yes/no question in indirect speech -> if / whether
   Comprehension: the PASSAGE is the only authority

HINDI
   Same vowel family -> DIRGH ; a+i=e -> GUNA ; visarg -> VISARG
   Number word + collection -> DVIGU
   Denotes a THIRD thing -> BAHUVRIHI
   Both equal, 'and' implied -> DVANDVA
   Adjective + noun, same referent -> KARMDHARAYA
   'se' meaning "by means of" = KARAN ; "from" = APADAN
   Error check order: ling, vachan, vibhakti, punarukti, word order

If you fall behind, the questions you sacrifice are the SLOW ones,
which you were least likely to get right anyway. That is the whole
point of this order.

8.4 A Final Word

8.4.1 What this book can and cannot do

I have tried to give you something honest rather than something impressive.

What this book does well. Parts 4, 5, 6 and 7 - Maths, Reasoning, English and Hindi - are 80 of the 100 marks, and they are finite and rule-based. Every formula you need is in Chapter 8.2. Every question type is in the chapters. If you work through them with a pen and practise against a clock, 74-78 out of those 80 is a repeatable outcome, not a lucky one. That is the real engine of a high score, and it is entirely within your control.

Part 2 covers static General Awareness thoroughly, including the Delhi-specific chapter that most candidates never touch and which is the cheapest differentiator available to you.

What this book cannot do. Part 3 explains this at length and I will not soften it here: current affairs cannot be delivered by a book. The events had not happened when this was written. I gave you a framework, a slot table to maintain, a verification method and a daily routine - but the daily fifteen minutes has to come from you. Nobody can do that part on your behalf.

One technical limitation, restated. The Hindi Part in this PDF is in transliteration because the PDF generator I wrote cannot render Devanagari, and no Devanagari font was available in the build environment. Use hindi_section_devanagari.html - open it in a browser and print to PDF - for your actual Hindi practice. Chapter 7.1 explains this fully.

8.4.2 The three things that actually decide it

  1. Section A deserves half your time, because it is half the paper. Almost nobody gives it that, and that is precisely why it is where selections are decided. Your competition is over-preparing their subject.
  1. Reasoning and Maths reward a pen, not eyes. Reading a solved example teaches nothing. Cover the solution, attempt it, then compare. This single habit separates candidates who score 19 from candidates who score 12 on identical knowledge.
  1. Start current affairs today, not next month. Fifteen minutes daily for six months covers the window your paper will draw from. Two weeks of panic covers about 5% of it.

Work steadily. Keep the leak list honest. Revise Part 8 until the formulas come without effort.

That is enough. All the best.
