7.2 Sandhi - Joining of Sounds

7.2.1 The concept

Sandhi means the merging of the last sound of one word with the first sound of the next, producing a changed form. There are three main types.

TypeRuleExample (transliterated)
Swar Sandhi (vowel)Vowel meets vowelvidya + arthi = vidyarthi
Vyanjan Sandhi (consonant)Consonant meets vowel or consonantsat + jan = sajjan
Visarg SandhiA word ending in the visarg (ah) meets another soundmanah + yog = manoyog

7.2.2 Swar Sandhi - the five sub-types

Sub-typeRuleExample
DirghSame vowels combine into their long form (a + a = aa, i + i = ee, u + u = oo)dharma + arth = dharmarth ; kavi + indra = kavindra ; bhanu + uday = bhanuday
Gunaa + i = e ; a + u = o ; a + ri = arnar + indra = narendra ; sur + ish = suresh ; maha + rishi = maharshi
Vriddhia + e = ai ; a + o = auek + ek = aikya ; jal + oormi = jalaurmi
Yani + dissimilar vowel becomes y ; u becomes vati + adhik = atyadhik ; su + agat = swagat
Ayadie + a = ay ; ai + a = aay ; o + a = av ; au + a = aavne + an = nayan ; pau + an = pavan

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] "Vidyarthi" is formed by which sandhi?

  • (a) Guna
  • (b) Vriddhi
  • (c) Dirgh <-- CORRECT
  • (d) Yan
Why: Break it: vidya + arthi. Here aa meets a, two similar vowels merging into a long aa - which is Dirgh sandhi. Rule to remember: when the two vowels are of the same family, it is always Dirgh.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] "Narendra" is an example of:

  • (a) Dirgh
  • (b) Guna <-- CORRECT
  • (c) Vriddhi
  • (d) Ayadi
Why: nar + indra. Here a + i = e, which is the defining pattern of Guna sandhi. Learn the three Guna combinations as a set: a+i=e, a+u=o, a+ri=ar.

Q. [EXPECTED] "Sajjan" is formed by which type of sandhi?

  • (a) Swar sandhi
  • (b) Vyanjan sandhi <-- CORRECT
  • (c) Visarg sandhi
  • (d) None
Why: sat + jan = sajjan. A consonant (t) meets a consonant (j) and changes, so this is Vyanjan sandhi. If either side of the join involves a consonant transformation, it is Vyanjan, not Swar.

Q. [EXPECTED] "Manoyog" involves which sandhi?

  • (a) Dirgh
  • (b) Guna
  • (c) Visarg <-- CORRECT
  • (d) Yan
Why: manah + yog. The first word ends in the visarg (ah), which is the signature of Visarg sandhi. Whenever you see ah / uh at the join, check Visarg first.

7.3 Samas - Compound Words

7.3.1 The six types

Samas is the combining of two or more words into one, dropping the connecting words. The type is decided by which part carries the main meaning.

TypeRule - which part dominatesExample (transliterated)
AvyayibhavThe first word dominates; the compound acts as an adverb. Often begins with prati, anu, bhar, yatha, aapratidin (every day), yathashakti (according to ability), bharpet (a full stomach)
TatpurushThe second word dominates; a case-ending is droppedrajkumar (raja ka kumar), dharmagranth (dharma ka granth), gyaneshwar
KarmdharayaOne word is an adjective describing the other; both refer to the same thingneelkamal (blue lotus), mahadev (great god), peetambar
DviguThe first word is a number; denotes a collectionnavratna (nine gems), chaturbhuj (four-armed), trilok (three worlds)
DvandvaBoth words are equally important; an 'and' is impliedmata-pita (mother and father), raat-din (night and day), dukh-sukh
BahuvrihiNeither word dominates; the compound denotes a third thing altogetherdashanan (one with ten faces, i.e. Ravana), lambodar (one with a large belly, i.e. Ganesha), chakradhar
TIP: How to identify the samas type in ten seconds

Ask the questions in this order:
1. Does it start with prati / anu / yatha / bhar / aa and act as an adverb? Then Avyayibhav.
2. Does the first word denote a number? Then Dvigu.
3. Do both words matter equally, with an implied "and"? Then Dvandva.
4. Does the compound refer to a third person or thing not named in either word? Then Bahuvrihi.
5. Is one word an adjective of the other, both pointing to the same thing? Then Karmdharaya.
6. Otherwise, if the second word dominates, it is Tatpurush.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] "Dashanan" is an example of which samas?

  • (a) Dvigu
  • (b) Karmdharaya
  • (c) Tatpurush
  • (d) Bahuvrihi <-- CORRECT
Why: "Dashanan" literally means "ten faces", but it actually denotes Ravana - a third entity named by neither component. That is the definition of Bahuvrihi. The trap is choosing Dvigu because "dash" is a number - but in Dvigu the compound denotes the *collection itself* (as in navratna, the nine gems), not a third thing.

Q. [PYQ-TYPE] "Mata-pita" belongs to which samas?

  • (a) Tatpurush
  • (b) Dvandva <-- CORRECT
  • (c) Dvigu
  • (d) Bahuvrihi
Why: Both words are equally important and an "and" is implied between them, which is Dvandva. Such compounds are usually written with a hyphen - a useful visual clue.

Q. [EXPECTED] "Pratidin" is an example of:

  • (a) Tatpurush
  • (b) Dvigu
  • (c) Avyayibhav <-- CORRECT
  • (d) Karmdharaya
Why: It begins with the prefix prati and functions as an adverb ("every day"), which marks Avyayibhav samas.

Q. [EXPECTED] "Neelkamal" is which samas?

  • (a) Dvandva
  • (b) Bahuvrihi
  • (c) Karmdharaya <-- CORRECT
  • (d) Dvigu
Why: "Neel" (blue) is an adjective describing "kamal" (lotus), and both words refer to the same object. That is Karmdharaya.

Q. [EXPECTED] In the word "nirdhan", the prefix is:

  • (a) nird
  • (b) nir <-- CORRECT
  • (c) dhan
  • (d) an
Why: nir is the prefix (meaning "without") and dhan (wealth) is the root, so nirdhan means "without wealth", i.e. poor. Related prefixes carrying negation: nir, nis, a, an, dur, apa.

Q. [EXPECTED] "Manavta" is formed by adding which suffix?

  • (a) -van
  • (b) -ik
  • (c) -ta <-- CORRECT
  • (d) -pan
Why: manav + ta. The suffix -ta converts a noun into an abstract noun (humanity). It is a tadhit pratyay because it attaches to a noun rather than a verb root.

7.5 Karak, Ling and Vachan

7.5.1 Karak - the eight cases

KarakEnglishMarker (vibhakti)
KartaSubject / doerne
KarmObjectko
KaranInstrument ("by means of")se, ke dwara
SampradanRecipient ("for")ke liye, ko
ApadanSeparation ("from")se (in the sense of separation)
SambandhPossessionka, ke, ki
AdhikaranLocationmein, par
SambodhanAddress / calling outhe, are, ho
TIP: The karak distinction that is always tested

Karan and Apadan both use the marker se, and telling them apart is the standard question.

Karan means "by means of" - an instrument. *Kalam se likha* (wrote with a pen).
Apadan means "from" - separation or origin. *Ped se patta gira* (the leaf fell from the tree).

Ask: is the se doing the work (Karan), or marking a source you are moving away from (Apadan)?

7.5.2 Ling and Vachan

  • Ling (gender) - Hindi has two: Pulling (masculine) and Stri-ling (feminine). Unlike English, inanimate objects also carry gender - *kitab* is feminine, *makan* is masculine.
  • Vachan (number) - Two: Ekvachan (singular) and Bahuvachan (plural). Common transformations: ladka to ladke, kitab to kitaben, nadi to nadiyan, chidiya to chidiyan.

7.6.1 Types of sentence by structure

7.6.3 Common errors tested in ashuddhi shodhan

Error typeWrong to Right (transliterated)

Vachan agreement"Ve ladka hai" to "Ve ladke hain"
Wrong vibhakti"Main school ko jata hun" to "Main school jata hun"

TIP: The two checks that catch most Hindi errors

Check 1 - gender agreement. Find the noun, decide its gender, then confirm every adjective and the verb agree with it. Most planted errors are gender mismatches, because English speakers do not naturally track the gender of inanimate objects.

Check 2 - the vibhakti. Verify that the case marker (ne, ko, se, mein, par) matches the karak the sentence actually needs. A wrong "ko" or "se" is the second most common planted error.

7.8 Apathit Gadyansh - Unseen Passage

7.8.1 The method

The comprehension technique is identical to English - only the language changes.

*THE METHOD*

Step 1: Read the QUESTIONS first, so you know what to look for.
Step 2: Read the passage once for the overall subject and tone.
Step 3: Answer factual questions by locating the exact line.
Step 4: Answer the "shirshak" (title) question LAST, after you have
        understood the whole passage.
Step 5: For a title, choose the option that covers the WHOLE passage -
        not one paragraph, and not something broader than the passage.

GOLDEN RULE: the answer must come from the PASSAGE. If an option is
true in general knowledge but is not stated in the passage, it is wrong.
TIP: The title question in Hindi comprehension

"Upyukt shirshak" (most appropriate title) questions almost always appear, and candidates lose them by choosing the most *interesting* option rather than the most *representative* one.

Test each option against two questions: does it cover the entire passage, and does it claim more than the passage says? The right answer covers everything and claims nothing extra.

7.9 Hindi Revision Sheet

7.9.1 The rules to memorise

*Hindi rules worth memorising*

SANDHI
   Same vowel family joining      -> DIRGH
   a + i = e ; a + u = o ; a + ri = ar  -> GUNA
   a + e = ai ; a + o = au        -> VRIDDHI
   i / u + dissimilar vowel becomes y / v -> YAN
   e, ai, o, au + vowel becomes ay, aay, av, aav -> AYADI
   Consonant transformation at the join -> VYANJAN
   First word ends in visarg (ah)  -> VISARG

SAMAS
   Adverb, starts with prati/anu/yatha/bhar -> AVYAYIBHAV
   First word is a NUMBER, denotes a collection -> DVIGU
   Both words equal, 'and' implied -> DVANDVA
   Denotes a THIRD thing not named  -> BAHUVRIHI
   One word is an adjective of the other -> KARMDHARAYA
   Second word dominates            -> TATPURUSH

KARAK MARKERS
   ne (karta), ko (karm), se/ke dwara (karan),
   ke liye (sampradan), se-separation (apadan),
   ka/ke/ki (sambandh), mein/par (adhikaran), he/are (sambodhan)

THE se DISTINCTION
   "by means of" = KARAN ;  "from / away from" = APADAN

ERROR CORRECTION - check in this order
   1. Gender agreement (ling)
   2. Number agreement (vachan)
   3. Case marker (vibhakti)
   4. Redundancy (punarukti dosh)
   5. Word order - the verb goes last

7.9.2 Habits that produce 17-19 out of 20

  1. Use the Devanagari HTML file for actual practice. This Part teaches the rules; the script must be practised in the script.
  2. Learn sandhi and samas by their identification rules, not by memorising word lists. The rules generalise; lists do not.
  3. For error correction, always check gender first. It is the most frequently planted error.
  4. Learn paryayvachi in family sets - all the words for sun together, all for moon together. They are asked as sets.
  5. Do the Hindi section first in the exam. For most candidates it is the fastest 20 marks in the paper, and banking it early settles the nerves.
