# PART 3 - General Awareness - Current Affairs

> *5 to 8 marks of the General Awareness section. This is the one Part of this book that cannot be complete, and this chapter explains exactly why and what to do about it.*



## 3.1 The Honest Chapter - Read This First



### 3.1.1 Why no book can give you current affairs


You asked me not to skip anything and to give this my best. The most useful thing I can do here is be straight with you rather than pad this Part with a list that looks impressive and expires.

Here is the problem in one sentence: **a book is written at one moment, and your exam happens at another.**


> **Every other Part of this book will still be correct in five years. Pythagoras will not change. Article 21A will not change. This Part starts decaying the day it is written.**


Three consequences you need to accept:

1. **I cannot predict specific current-affairs questions.** Anyone who claims to is guessing and dressing it up. What I *can* do is show you the **categories** that recur every single year, and those categories are genuinely stable.

2. **Some facts in this Part will already be out of date when you read it.** Office holders change. Tournaments get won by someone else. Where I give a specific name or figure, I have flagged how to verify it.

3. **A daily habit will beat this chapter.** Fifteen minutes a day for six months is worth more than this entire Part, because it covers the actual window your paper draws from.


> **NOTE: What I did and did not verify**
>
> I searched for current affairs material while writing this and found the standard aggregator sources, monthly capsules and summit round-ups. But I want to be precise about the limits of that:
>
> **I have not independently verified individual current-affairs facts** against primary government sources, and current-affairs aggregators contain errors. So rather than transcribing a list of names and dates that I cannot stand behind and that you would be memorising on trust, I have built this Part as a **framework plus a verification method**.
>
> Where I do name something specific, I say where to check it. Where I am not confident, I say so instead of filling the gap. A wrong fact learned confidently is worse than a gap you know you have - in an exam with negative marking, false confidence actively costs you marks.
>
> Sources consulted for the framework and category structure:

> [Vajiram and Ravi - summits and conferences](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/important-summits-and-conferences/) ,

### 3.1.2 What the examiner actually asks


This is the part that *is* predictable, and it is worth more than a list of facts. Across DSSSB and similar papers, current-affairs questions cluster into a small number of shapes.


| Category | Typical question | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Appointments | Who was appointed CJI / RBI Governor / Chief Election Commissioner / Army Chief? | High |
| Government schemes | Which ministry launched scheme X? What is its objective? | High |
| Sports | Who won a major tournament? Where were the last Olympics / Asian Games held? Where are the next? | High |
| Awards | Who received the Bharat Ratna / Jnanpith / Dadasaheb Phalke / Nobel this year? | High |
| Summits and conferences | Which country hosted the G20 / BRICS / SCO / COP summit? What was its theme? | Medium |
| Reports and indices | Which organisation publishes the Human Development Index / Global Hunger Index / Ease of Doing Business? | Medium |
| Defence | Which missile was test-fired? Which warship was commissioned? What was the exercise called? | Medium |
| Space and science | Recent ISRO missions and their objectives | Medium |
| Books and authors | Who wrote a recently released notable book? | Low |
| Obituaries | Notable deaths and what the person was known for | Low |
| Days and themes | The theme of a major international day this year | Low |
| State news | New capitals, new districts, state schemes - especially **Delhi** | Medium for DSSSB |



> **TIP: The single most valuable insight in this Part**
>
> Notice that **most of those categories are questions about a slot, not about an event.** "Who is the RBI Governor" is a slot that always exists; only the occupant changes. "Which country hosted the G20" is a slot; only the host changes.
>
> So you do not need to read everything. You need to **maintain a small table of slots** and keep the occupants current. That table is perhaps 60 lines long, and it covers the majority of appointment-and-position questions.
>
> I have given you the table structure in Chapter 3.2. Filling it in is a 30-minute job, and updating it is a 5-minute monthly job.

**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] The Human Development Index is published by which organisation?**

- (a) World Bank
- (b) IMF
- (c) **UNDP  <-- CORRECT**
- (d) WEF

> **Why:** The **UNDP** publishes the HDI, which combines life expectancy, education and per capita income. Note the frequent distractors: the **World Bank** publishes the World Development Report, the **WEF** publishes the Global Competitiveness and Gender Gap reports, and the **IMF** publishes the World Economic Outlook. The **publisher** is the stable, examinable fact; the rank changes yearly.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The Global Hunger Index is jointly published by:**

- (a) UNDP and WHO
- (b) **Concern Worldwide and Welthungerhilfe  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) FAO and WFP
- (d) World Bank and IMF

> **Why:** This one is commonly misattributed to a UN body. It is published by two NGOs - **Concern Worldwide** (Ireland) and **Welthungerhilfe** (Germany).

**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] The Swachh Bharat Mission was launched on:**

- (a) 15 August 2014
- (b) **2 October 2014  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) 26 January 2015
- (d) 2 October 2015

> **Why:** Launched on **2 October 2014**, Gandhi's birth anniversary, which is the mnemonic - a cleanliness mission launched on Gandhi Jayanti.



**Q. [EXPECTED] PM POSHAN is the new name of which scheme?**

- (a) Ayushman Bharat
- (b) **Mid-Day Meal Scheme  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Jan Dhan Yojana
- (d) Ujjwala Yojana

> **Why:** The **Mid-Day Meal Scheme** was renamed **PM POSHAN** (Poshan Shakti Nirman). Do not confuse it with **ICDS**, which serves children **under 6** through Anganwadis, whereas PM POSHAN serves **school-going** children.



## 3.4 Your Daily Current Affairs System



### 3.4.1 The 15-minute routine



*THE DAILY ROUTINE - 15 minutes, every day, no exceptions*
```
MINUTES 1-8   : Read one reliable daily current-affairs digest.
                Do NOT read a newspaper cover to cover - it is not an
                efficient use of exam preparation time.

MINUTES 9-13  : Write into your notebook ONLY items that fit a slot
                or a category from Chapter 3.1.2:
                   appointment / scheme / sport / award / summit /
                   report / defence / space / Delhi
                One line each. Date on the left.
                If an item fits no category, SKIP IT.

MINUTES 14-15 : Re-read yesterday's lines and last Sunday's lines.
                This is the spaced repetition that makes it stick.

WEEKLY  (20 min) : Re-read the whole week. Update your slot table.
MONTHLY (40 min) : Re-read the month. Attempt a monthly current-affairs
                   quiz to find gaps.
FINAL 30 DAYS    : Read ONLY your own notebook, plus a last-6-months
                   compilation. Do not start new sources at this stage.
```



### 3.4.2 What to read



| Type of source | Why |
|---|---|
| A daily current-affairs digest aimed at competitive exams | Pre-filtered for exam relevance; saves hours over reading a full newspaper |
| One monthly current-affairs compilation | Catches what you missed and consolidates |
| PIB (pib.gov.in) press releases | **The primary source** for government schemes and appointments. If a digest and PIB disagree, PIB is right |
| delhi.gov.in and edudel.nic.in | Delhi-specific news that national sources omit - your differentiator for DSSSB |
| A last-6-months compilation, in the final month | The highest-yield single revision document before the exam |



> **NOTE: The mistake that costs candidates the most marks**
>
> The commonest failure is **starting current affairs two weeks before the exam**. It cannot work, because current-affairs questions are drawn from a window of roughly six to twelve months. Two weeks of cramming covers perhaps 5% of that window.
>
> The second commonest failure is the opposite: **reading everything and retaining nothing**. Candidates spend an hour daily on newspapers and remember none of it, because they never wrote anything down and never revised.
>
> The routine above fixes both. It is short enough to sustain for months and structured enough to retain. **Fifteen disciplined minutes daily beats two undisciplined hours.**



### 3.4.3 A realistic expectation for this section



| Your current-affairs preparation | Realistic score out of the 5-8 CA questions |
|---|---|
| No preparation at all | 1-2 (pure luck) |
| Two weeks of cramming before the exam | 2-3 |
| Three months of the daily routine | 4-5 |
| Six months or more of the daily routine, with the slot table maintained | 5-7 |


Combine that with the static GK in Part 2 - which is fully learnable and worth 12-15 of the 20 - and you can see how the section adds up:


> **Static GK done properly (12-15) + current affairs done properly (5-7) = 17-20 out of 20. Static GK alone caps you at about 15. That is precisely why this Part exists, and why it cannot be skipped.**



## 3.5 Current Affairs Practice - Question Patterns



### 3.5.1 Questions built on the stable parts


The questions below test the **structural** knowledge that does not expire - publishers, ministries, mandates, host-rotation patterns. These are the current-affairs marks you can actually secure in advance.


**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Which organisation publishes the World Economic Outlook?**

- (a) World Bank
- (b) **IMF  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) WEF
- (d) UNCTAD

> **Why:** The **IMF** publishes the World Economic Outlook twice yearly. Contrast the **World Bank's** World Development Report. Publisher-to-report matching is the most reliably examinable current-affairs knowledge.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The Economic Survey of India is presented by which ministry, and when?**

- (a) Ministry of Commerce, in March
- (b) **Ministry of Finance, a day before the Union Budget  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) NITI Aayog, in April
- (d) RBI, in January

> **Why:** The **Ministry of Finance** (prepared by the Chief Economic Adviser) tables it usually the day before the Budget, which is presented on **1 February**.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The Nobel Prize in which field is NOT awarded by a Swedish institution?**

- (a) Physics
- (b) Chemistry
- (c) Literature
- (d) **Peace  <-- CORRECT**

> **Why:** The **Nobel Peace Prize** is awarded by the **Norwegian Nobel Committee in Oslo**; all others are awarded in Sweden. Also note the **Economics** prize was added in **1969** and is funded by Sweden's central bank, not by Nobel's original will.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The Bharat Ratna was instituted in which year?**

- (a) 1950
- (b) **1954  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) 1947
- (d) 1960

> **Why:** **1954**, alongside the Padma awards. It is India's highest civilian honour and can be awarded posthumously. A maximum of three may be conferred in a year.



**Q. [EXPECTED] Which body conducts the census of India?**

- (a) NITI Aayog
- (b) **Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, Ministry of Home Affairs  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Ministry of Statistics
- (d) Election Commission

> **Why:** Conducted by the **Registrar General and Census Commissioner** under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**. India's first complete census was **1881**, and it has been decennial since.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The theme-based international days are announced by which body?**

- (a) Individual national governments
- (b) **The United Nations and its specialised agencies  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) WEF
- (d) BRICS

> **Why:** The **UN** and agencies such as WHO and UNESCO designate observance days and set annual themes. **The day itself is static and learnable** (5 June is World Environment Day); only the **theme** changes annually - so learn the dates now and pick up the themes from your daily reading.
