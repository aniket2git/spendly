# PART 1 - The Section A Pattern and Your Strategy

> *100 marks, five sections, twenty questions each. Identical for every DSSSB post. Read this Part before you study a single topic.*



## 1.1 The Pattern, Verified



### 1.1.1 What Section A actually is


Every DSSSB recruitment paper - TGT, PGT, PRT, LDC, DASS, Junior Engineer, ASO, Nursing Officer, Scientific Assistant, Special Educator - shares one common half. That half is **Section A**.


| Section A component | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| General Awareness | 20 | 20 |
| General Intelligence and Reasoning Ability | 20 | 20 |
| Arithmetical and Numerical Ability | 20 | 20 |
| Test of English Language and Comprehension | 20 | 20 |
| Test of Hindi Language and Comprehension | 20 | 20 |
| **TOTAL SECTION A** | **100** | **100** |



| Whole paper | Marks |
|---|---|
| Section A (the five components above) | 100 |
| Section B (post-specific subject + teaching methodology, or the technical paper) | 100 |
| **TOTAL** | **200** |


- **Duration** - **2 hours (120 minutes)** for 200 questions. That is **36 seconds per question** on average.
- **Marking** - **+1** for a correct answer, **-0.25** for a wrong answer, **0** for a skipped question.
- **Mode** - Computer Based Test (CBT), objective multiple choice with four options.
- **Language** - Bilingual (English and Hindi), except the two language-test sections themselves.

### 1.1.2 Why Section A is where selections are won


Think about what these two halves are.

**Section B** is your subject. You studied it for three years at degree level. Every other candidate in the hall also studied it for three years. The marks there cluster tightly - most serious candidates score somewhere between 60 and 80, and pushing from 70 to 75 costs enormous effort.

**Section A** is the opposite. It is broad, shallow and highly repetitive. And most candidates barely touch it, because it feels less serious than their subject. The spread here is enormous - I have seen candidates score 35 and candidates score 88 on the same paper.


> **That spread is the selection. A candidate who scores 70 in Section B and 85 in Section A beats a candidate who scores 80 in Section B and 55 in Section A - by 20 marks.**



| Reality | Consequence for you |
|---|---|
| Section A is exactly half the paper | It deserves close to half your preparation time. Almost nobody gives it that |
| It is identical across posts | Study it once and it serves any DSSSB exam you ever sit |
| Four of the five sections are pure technique or pure recall | They are trainable in weeks, unlike a subject which takes years |
| The topics repeat with unusual fidelity | Last year's topic list is next year's topic list, with the current affairs swapped out |



### 1.1.3 What "95+" actually requires


You asked for a book that gets a candidate past 95. Let me be precise and honest about what that means, because a vague promise would not help you.

**95 out of 100 in Section A is possible but demanding.** Here is the realistic route:


| Section | Target | Why this target is realistic |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 19-20 / 20 | Pure technique. With practice, near-full marks is normal. There is no "unknown" in reasoning - every question type is finite and learnable |
| Mathematics | 18-20 / 20 | Also technique. The topic list is closed and the difficulty is Class 10 level. Speed is the only real constraint |
| English | 17-19 / 20 | Grammar and error-spotting are rule-based. Vocabulary is the one open-ended part |
| Hindi | 17-19 / 20 | Same as English - vyakaran is rule-based and scoring |
| General Awareness | 13-17 / 20 | **The honest weak point.** Static GK is learnable, but current affairs is open-ended. Nobody reliably scores 20 here |


Add the realistic middles: 19 + 19 + 18 + 18 + 15 = **89**. Add the optimistic ends: 20 + 20 + 19 + 19 + 17 = **95**.


> **NOTE: The honest version of the "95+" promise**
>
> **Reasoning, Maths, English and Hindi are 80 marks, and they are almost entirely within your control.** They are rule-based and finite. If you work through Parts 4, 5, 6 and 7 of this book properly and practise, scoring **74-78 out of those 80** is a realistic, repeatable outcome. That is the real engine of a high score.
>
> **General Awareness is 20 marks and only partly within your control.** Static GK - history, geography, polity, economy, science - is learnable and this book covers it thoroughly. **Current affairs is not learnable from any book**, including this one, because the events have not happened yet when the book is written. You must add a daily source. I explain exactly how in Chapter 3.1, and I will not pretend otherwise.
>
> So the honest promise is this: **this book can take you to about 90, and a disciplined current-affairs habit on top of it can take you past 95.** Any book that claims to do the whole 95 by itself, current affairs included, is not being straight with you.



## 1.2 Time Strategy Inside the Exam Hall



### 1.2.1 The 36-second problem


200 questions in 120 minutes is 36 seconds each. But the questions are not equal. A synonym takes 8 seconds. A time-and-work sum takes 90. A reading comprehension passage takes 4 minutes for 5 questions.

So you do not budget per question. You budget **per section**.


| Section | Questions | Suggested time | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hindi Language | 20 | 10 minutes | Fastest for most Indian candidates. Bank these marks first |
| English Language | 20 | 12 minutes | Grammar and vocabulary are instant; only the passage takes time |
| General Awareness | 20 | 8 minutes | You either know it or you do not. Never think about a GA question - answer or skip in 20 seconds |
| Reasoning | 20 | 20 minutes | Some types (puzzles, series) genuinely need working time |
| Mathematics | 20 | 25 minutes | The slowest section. Do the formula-based ones first, leave the word problems |
| **Section A subtotal** | **100** | **75 minutes** | - |
| Section B (your subject) | 100 | **42 minutes** | Your subject is recall-heavy, so it moves faster than maths |
| Buffer / review | - | 3 minutes | Check for OMR or marking errors |



> **TIP: The order to attempt in**
>
> Do **not** attempt the paper top to bottom. Attempt it in **increasing order of time cost**:
>
> **Hindi, then English, then General Awareness, then your Section B subject, then Reasoning, then Mathematics.**
>
> Two reasons. First, you bank the certain marks before fatigue and clock-panic set in. Second, if you run out of time - which most candidates do - the questions you sacrifice are the slow ones you were least likely to get right anyway, not the fast ones you were certain about.



### 1.2.2 The two-pass method



*How to move through a section*
```
PASS 1  (fast sweep)
  - Read each question once.
  - If the answer arrives within your time budget, mark it and move on.
  - If it does not, FLAG IT and move on immediately. Do not linger.
  - Target: finish pass 1 using about 70% of the section's time.

PASS 2  (return sweep)
  - Go back only to the flagged questions.
  - For each, try to ELIMINATE two options.
  - Succeeded? Answer it - the expected value is positive.
  - Failed? Leave it blank and move on.

NEVER let one question consume more than 2 minutes. Ever.
```



### 1.2.3 The mathematics of guessing - learn this table


With +1 and -0.25, guessing is not free but it is not forbidden either. The decision is arithmetic, not courage.


| Your situation | Chance of being right | Expected marks | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| You know it | 100% | +1.00 | **Answer** |
| You eliminated 3 of 4 (certain of two, unsure which) | 50% | 0.5(1) + 0.5(-0.25) = **+0.375** | **Answer - clearly worth it** |
| You eliminated 2 of 4 | 33% | 0.33(1) + 0.67(-0.25) = **+0.16** | **Answer - still positive** |
| You eliminated nothing (blind guess) | 25% | 0.25(1) + 0.75(-0.25) = **+0.0625** | Marginal. **Skip** if any distractor looks deliberately attractive |
| You have a hunch but cannot eliminate anything | below 25% | Negative | **Skip** |



> **The rule that follows: never leave blank a question where you can eliminate two options. Never attempt a question where you can eliminate none.**



> **NOTE: Why blind guessing is worse than the maths suggests**
>
> The +0.0625 figure assumes each of the four options is equally likely - a random lottery. Real exam papers are not random. Examiners deliberately build **attractive wrong options** that match a common misconception. So on a question you genuinely do not understand, your real chance of being right is often *below* 25%, because you will be drawn to the trap rather than picking uniformly.
>
> That is why the practical rule is: **eliminate, or skip.** And it is why the "why the wrong options are wrong" explanations throughout this book matter more than the answers themselves - they are training your elimination instinct.



## 1.3 How to Use This Book



### 1.3.1 The study sequence



| Week | Focus | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Part 5 (Reasoning) - all chapters, every type | Recognise every question type on sight |
| 3-4 | Part 4 (Mathematics) - formulas first, then chapters | Learn every formula in Chapter 4.1 by heart |
| 5 | Part 6 (English) - grammar rules, then vocabulary | Finish all rule chapters |
| 6 | Part 7 (Hindi) - vyakaran | Finish all rule chapters |
| 7-9 | Part 2 (General Awareness) - one chapter per two days | One full pass of static GK |
| 10 | Part 3 (Current Affairs) - set up your daily habit | Habit running, notes started |
| 11-12 | Revision: Part 8 only, plus daily current affairs | Two full passes of Part 8 |
| Ongoing from week 1 | **15 minutes of current affairs every single day** | Non-negotiable |



### 1.3.2 The rules that make this work


1. **Reasoning and Maths need a pen, not just eyes.** Reading a solved example teaches you nothing. Cover the solution, attempt it, then compare. This is the single biggest difference between candidates who score 19 and candidates who score 12.

2. **Keep a leak list.** One line per mistake, naming the **concept**, not the question. "Forgot that percentage change uses the original as base." After each Part, that list is your revision syllabus.

3. **Static GK needs spaced repetition, not long sessions.** Twenty minutes daily beats four hours weekly. Facts decay; revisit them.

4. **Start current affairs on day one, not month three.** Six months of 15-minute daily reading is worth more than two weeks of panic revision, because current affairs questions span the whole year.

5. **Time yourself from week 3.** Accuracy without speed fails a 36-second-per-question paper. Practise chapters against a clock.
