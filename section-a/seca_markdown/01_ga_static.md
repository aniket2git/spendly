## 2.1 How to Study This Part



### 2.1.1 What the syllabus says, and what that means


The official DSSSB syllabus describes General Awareness as testing awareness of the environment around the candidate and its application to society, including **current events** and matters of **everyday observation and experience** in their scientific aspect, with questions from **History, Geography, General Science, Economics, Polity and scientific research**.

That is deliberately broad. But real papers concentrate heavily, and the concentration is predictable.

| Indian History | 2-3 | HIGH |

> **TIP: How to actually retain this Part**
>
> Static GK is defeated by **spaced repetition**, not by long sessions. Twenty minutes daily beats four hours on a Sunday, because facts decay on a predictable curve and each revisit flattens that curve.
>
> Practical method: read one chapter, then re-read only its **tables** on day 3, day 10 and day 30. The tables in this Part were written to be revisable in isolation for exactly this reason.



## 2.2 Indian Polity and Constitution



### 2.2.1 The Constitution - core facts



| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Constituent Assembly formed | 1946, under the Cabinet Mission Plan |
| Chairman of the Constituent Assembly | Dr. Rajendra Prasad |
| Chairman of the Drafting Committee | **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** - called the Father of the Indian Constitution |
| Constitution adopted | **26 November 1949** (celebrated as Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas) |
| Constitution came into force | **26 January 1950** (Republic Day) |
| Time taken | 2 years, 11 months and 18 days |
| Original Constitution | 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules |
| Presently | Around 470 Articles, 25 Parts, **12 Schedules** |
| Longest written constitution in the world | Yes |
| Borrowed features | Parliamentary system and rule of law from **Britain**; Fundamental Rights, judicial review and impeachment from the **USA**; Directive Principles from **Ireland**; Fundamental Duties and five-year plans from the **USSR**; Emergency provisions from **Germany**; concurrent list from **Australia**; federation with a strong centre from **Canada** |



### 2.2.2 Fundamental Rights, Duties and Directive Principles



| Right | Articles |
|---|---|
| Right to Equality | 14 to 18 |
| Right to Freedom | 19 to 22 |
| Right against Exploitation | 23 to 24 |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25 to 28 |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | 29 to 30 |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | **32** - called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Ambedkar |


- **Originally seven Fundamental Rights** - The **Right to Property** was removed by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and made an ordinary legal right under Article 300A. Six remain.
- **Article 21A** - **Right to Education** for children aged 6-14, added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**.
- **Fundamental Duties** - Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**. Originally 10; an 11th (duty of a parent to provide education) was added by the **86th Amendment**. Listed in **Article 51A**, Part IVA.
- **Directive Principles** - Part IV, Articles 36-51. Borrowed from **Ireland**. They are **not enforceable** in a court.
- **The five writs** - **Habeas Corpus** (produce the body), **Mandamus** (we command), **Prohibition**, **Certiorari**, **Quo Warranto**.

### 2.2.4 Parliament and the executive



| Office / Body | Key facts |
|---|---|
| President | Head of state. Elected indirectly by an electoral college. Minimum age 35. Term 5 years. Article 52-62 |
| Vice-President | Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Minimum age 35 |
| Prime Minister | Real executive head. Appointed by the President; leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha |
| Lok Sabha | Maximum strength 552. Term 5 years. Minimum age to contest 25. Presided over by the **Speaker** |
| Rajya Sabha | Maximum strength 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated). A **permanent house** - never dissolved; one-third retire every 2 years. Term 6 years. Minimum age 30 |
| Money Bill | Can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, and only with the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can delay it for at most **14 days** |
| Joint sitting | Held for an ordinary bill deadlock; presided over by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**. Not applicable to Money Bills or Constitutional Amendment Bills |
| Supreme Court | Established 1950. Currently 34 judges including the CJI. Judges retire at **65** |
| High Court judges | Retire at **62** |
| Attorney General | The government's chief legal adviser - Article 76 |
| CAG | Comptroller and Auditor General - Article 148, called the guardian of the public purse |
| Election Commission | Article 324. A multi-member body |
| UPSC | Article 315 |
| Finance Commission | Article 280, constituted every 5 years |
| NITI Aayog | Replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015. The Prime Minister is its Chairperson |



**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Who is called the Father of the Indian Constitution?**

- (a) Jawaharlal Nehru
- (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
- (c) **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar  <-- CORRECT**
- (d) Mahatma Gandhi

> **Why:** Ambedkar chaired the **Drafting Committee**. Note the distinction that examiners exploit: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was the President of the **Constituent Assembly**, and later India's first President.



**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Which Article of the Constitution deals with the Right to Education?**

- (a) Article 19
- (b) **Article 21A  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Article 32
- (d) Article 45

> **Why:** **Article 21A**, inserted by the **86th Amendment, 2002**, makes free and compulsory education for ages 6-14 a fundamental right. **Article 45** is the related *Directive Principle* on early childhood care - a deliberate distractor.



**Q. [EXPECTED] [TRAP] Which Fundamental Right was removed from the Constitution?**

- (a) Right to Equality
- (b) **Right to Property  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Right to Freedom
- (d) Right against Exploitation

> **Why:** The **Right to Property** was deleted as a Fundamental Right by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and shifted to **Article 300A** as an ordinary legal right. So India now has **six** Fundamental Rights, not seven.



**Q. [EXPECTED] The Rajya Sabha is a permanent house because:**

- (a) Its members serve for life
- (b) **It is never dissolved; one-third of members retire every two years  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) It has no elections
- (d) The President chairs it

> **Why:** Its continuity is the key contrast with the Lok Sabha, which can be dissolved. Members serve **6-year** terms staggered so a third retire biennially.



## 2.3 Indian History



### 2.3.1 Ancient India



| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Indus Valley Civilisation | About 2500-1750 BCE. Bronze Age. Major sites: **Harappa** (Punjab, Pakistan, on the Ravi), **Mohenjodaro** (Sindh, on the Indus, means "mound of the dead"), **Dholavira** and **Lothal** (Gujarat - Lothal had a dockyard), **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan), **Rakhigarhi** (Haryana, the largest Indian site). Known for town planning, grid streets, drainage, the Great Bath at Mohenjodaro. The script is undeciphered |
| Vedic Period | Four Vedas: **Rigveda** (oldest, hymns), **Samaveda** (music), **Yajurveda** (sacrificial formulae), **Atharvaveda** (spells and medicine). "Satyameva Jayate" comes from the **Mundaka Upanishad** |
| Buddhism | Founded by **Gautama Buddha** (563-483 BCE), born at **Lumbini** (Nepal), enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya**, first sermon at **Sarnath**, died at **Kushinagar**. Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path. Texts in **Pali** |
| Jainism | 24th Tirthankara **Mahavira** (599-527 BCE), born at Kundagrama near Vaishali. First Tirthankara **Rishabhanatha**. Principles: non-violence, truth, non-stealing, celibacy, non-possession |
| Mauryan Empire | Founded by **Chandragupta Maurya** (321 BCE) with **Chanakya (Kautilya)** as adviser, author of the **Arthashastra**. **Ashoka** (268-232 BCE) fought the **Kalinga war** (261 BCE) and embraced Buddhism. Ashoka's edicts are in **Prakrit** in **Brahmi** script. The **Sarnath Lion Capital** is India's national emblem |
| Gupta Empire | Called the **Golden Age** of India. **Chandragupta I**, **Samudragupta** (the Indian Napoleon), **Chandragupta II Vikramaditya**. **Aryabhata**, **Kalidasa**, **Varahamihira**, **Brahmagupta** belong to this era |



### 2.3.2 Medieval India



| Dynasty / Event | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Delhi Sultanate | 1206-1526. Five dynasties: **Slave (Mamluk)**, **Khilji**, **Tughlaq**, **Sayyid**, **Lodi** |
| Qutbuddin Aibak | Founder of the Slave dynasty; began the **Qutub Minar** |
| Iltutmish | Introduced the silver **tanka** and copper **jital** |
| Razia Sultan | The only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate |
| Alauddin Khilji | Market control reforms; built **Siri Fort** and **Alai Darwaza** |
| Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Shifted the capital to **Daulatabad**; introduced token currency - both failed |
| Firoz Shah Tughlaq | Built **Firozabad**; installed Ashokan pillars in Delhi |
| First Battle of Panipat | **1526** - Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi, founding the **Mughal Empire** |
| Second Battle of Panipat | 1556 - Akbar's forces defeated Hemu |
| Third Battle of Panipat | 1761 - Marathas defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali |
| Akbar | 1556-1605. **Din-i-Ilahi**, abolished jizya, **Mansabdari** system. Navratnas included Birbal, Tansen, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl |
| Shah Jahan | Built the **Taj Mahal** (Agra), the **Red Fort** and **Jama Masjid** (Delhi), and moved the capital to **Shahjahanabad** |
| Aurangzeb | Last powerful Mughal; reimposed jizya |
| Battle of Plassey | **1757** - Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daula; the beginning of British political power in India |
| Battle of Buxar | 1764 - established British control over Bengal, Bihar and Odisha |
| Shivaji | Founder of the Maratha empire; crowned at Raigad in 1674 |
| Vijayanagara Empire | Founded 1336 by Harihara and Bukka; **Krishnadevaraya** its greatest ruler; capital **Hampi** |

## 2.5 General Science



### 2.5.1 Physics

**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Which part of the cell is known as the powerhouse of the cell?**

- (a) Nucleus
- (b) Ribosome
- (c) **Mitochondria  <-- CORRECT**
- (d) Lysosome

> **Why:** **Mitochondria** carry out cellular respiration and generate **ATP**, the cell's energy currency. Learn the family: nucleus = brain, **lysosome = suicidal bags**, ribosome = protein factory, chloroplast = kitchen (in plants).



**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Malaria is caused by which organism, and spread by which vector?**

- (a) Bacteria; housefly
- (b) **Protozoa (Plasmodium); female Anopheles mosquito  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Virus; Aedes mosquito
- (d) Fungus; sandfly

> **Why:** Malaria is caused by the **protozoan Plasmodium** and transmitted by the **female Anopheles**. Note the contrast: **dengue and chikungunya** are viral and spread by the **Aedes aegypti** mosquito.



**Q. [EXPECTED] Which gas is most abundant in the Earth's atmosphere?**

- (a) Oxygen
- (b) **Nitrogen  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Carbon dioxide
- (d) Argon

> **Why:** **Nitrogen at about 78%**, with oxygen about 21% and argon under 1%. Carbon dioxide is only about 0.04% despite its climatic importance.



**Q. [EXPECTED] [TRAP] Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles, and why?**

- (a) Concave, because it magnifies
- (b) **Convex, because it gives a wider field of view  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Plane, because it shows true size
- (d) Concave, because it shows a real image

> **Why:** A **convex** mirror diverges light, producing a small, erect virtual image and covering a **wider field of view**. **Concave** mirrors converge light and are used in torches, headlights and shaving mirrors.



## 2.6 Indian Economy



| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| RBI | Established **1 April 1935**; nationalised 1949. India's central bank and the sole authority to issue currency **except the one-rupee coin and notes**, which the Ministry of Finance issues |
| Monetary policy tools | Repo rate, reverse repo rate, CRR, SLR, open market operations |
| Inflation types | **Demand-pull** (demand exceeds supply) and **cost-push** (rising input costs). Measured by **CPI** and **WPI** |
| GDP vs GNP | GDP counts output produced within the country; GNP adds net factor income from abroad |
| Fiscal deficit | Total expenditure minus total revenue excluding borrowings |
| Budget | Presented on **1 February**; the financial year runs 1 April to 31 March. Article 112 calls it the Annual Financial Statement |
| GST | Introduced **1 July 2017**; a destination-based indirect tax with slabs. The GST Council is chaired by the Union Finance Minister |
| Direct vs indirect tax | Direct: income tax, corporate tax. Indirect: GST, customs duty |
| NITI Aayog | Replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015 |

## 2.8 Delhi-Specific General Knowledge



### 2.8.1 Why this chapter exists


DSSSB recruits for the **Government of the National Capital Territory of Delhi**. Questions about Delhi - its administration, history, monuments and civic bodies - appear in DSSSB papers in a way they never do in a national exam. Most candidates never prepare this, which makes it cheap marks.


| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Status | **National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi** - a Union Territory with a legislature, under the **69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991** |
| Governing Articles | **239AA and 239AB** |
| Executive head | The **Lieutenant Governor (LG)**, appointed by the President |
| Head of government | The **Chief Minister**, leading the Council of Ministers |
| Legislative Assembly | 70 seats; term 5 years |
| Lok Sabha seats from Delhi | **7** |
| Rajya Sabha seats from Delhi | **3** |
| Subjects outside the Assembly's power | **Public order, police and land** - these rest with the Union Government |
| High Court | Delhi High Court, established **1966** |
| Municipal body | **Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD)** - the three corporations were reunified into a single MCD in **2022**. Also the New Delhi Municipal Council (NDMC) and the Delhi Cantonment Board |
| Districts | 11 revenue districts |



### 2.8.2 Delhi's history and monuments



| Monument / Site | Facts |
|---|---|
| Qutub Minar | Begun by **Qutbuddin Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish**. Tallest brick minaret in the world. **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. The Iron Pillar in its complex is rust-resistant |
| Red Fort (Lal Qila) | Built by **Shah Jahan** (1638-1648) at Shahjahanabad. **UNESCO site**. The Prime Minister addresses the nation from here on Independence Day |
| Humayun's Tomb | Built by Humayun's widow **Bega Begum**; the first garden-tomb in India and an architectural precursor to the Taj Mahal. **UNESCO site** |
| Jama Masjid | Built by Shah Jahan; one of India's largest mosques |
| India Gate | War memorial by **Edwin Lutyens**, honouring soldiers of the First World War. The National War Memorial was inaugurated nearby in 2019 |
| Rashtrapati Bhavan | Designed by **Edwin Lutyens**; the President's residence |
| Parliament House | Designed by **Herbert Baker**. The new Parliament building was inaugurated in 2023 |
| Lotus Temple | Bahai House of Worship |
| Akshardham | Swaminarayan temple complex |
| Purana Qila | Associated with Humayun and Sher Shah Suri |
| Jantar Mantar | Astronomical observatory built by **Sawai Jai Singh II** |
| Raj Ghat | Memorial to Mahatma Gandhi |


- **The seven cities of Delhi** - Traditionally: **Qila Rai Pithora** (Tomars/Chauhans), **Siri** (Alauddin Khilji), **Tughlaqabad** (Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq), **Jahanpanah** (Muhammad bin Tughlaq), **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Tughlaq), **Dinpanah/Purana Qila** (Humayun and Sher Shah), and **Shahjahanabad** (Shah Jahan).
- **Capital shifted to Delhi** - Announced at the **Delhi Durbar of 1911**; New Delhi formally inaugurated in **1931**.
- **Delhi Metro** - Operated by **DMRC**, established 1995; first line opened in **2002** (Shahdara to Tis Hazari). E. Sreedharan is called the **Metro Man of India**.
- **Rivers and geography** - The **Yamuna** flows through Delhi; the **Aravalli** range's northern extension (the Delhi Ridge) runs through it.
- **Delhi's UNESCO World Heritage Sites** - **Qutub Minar complex, Red Fort complex, Humayun's Tomb.**


**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Under which Constitutional Amendment did Delhi get the status of National Capital Territory?**

- (a) 42nd Amendment
- (b) **69th Amendment  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) 73rd Amendment
- (d) 74th Amendment

> **Why:** The **69th Amendment Act, 1991** inserted **Articles 239AA and 239AB**, creating the NCT of Delhi with a Legislative Assembly and Council of Ministers. Note the distractors: the **73rd** is panchayats and the **74th** is municipalities.



**Q. [PYQ-TYPE] Which three subjects are outside the legislative competence of the Delhi Legislative Assembly?**

- (a) Health, education, transport
- (b) **Public order, police and land  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Water, electricity, housing
- (d) Finance, taxation, excise

> **Why:** **Public order, police and land** remain with the Union Government - the defining limitation of Delhi's special status and a very likely DSSSB question.



**Q. [EXPECTED] Who designed Rashtrapati Bhavan?**

- (a) Herbert Baker
- (b) **Edwin Lutyens  <-- CORRECT**
- (c) Le Corbusier
- (d) Charles Correa

> **Why:** **Edwin Lutyens** designed Rashtrapati Bhavan and India Gate; **Herbert Baker** designed the Secretariat blocks and the old Parliament House. The pair is a standard distractor set.



**Q. [EXPECTED] How many Lok Sabha seats does Delhi have?**

- (a) 5
- (b) 6
- (c) **7  <-- CORRECT**
- (d) 10

> **Why:** **7 Lok Sabha** seats and **3 Rajya Sabha** seats, with **70** Assembly constituencies.
