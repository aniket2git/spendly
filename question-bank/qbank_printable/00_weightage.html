*Where the marks actually are. Read this before you attempt a single question.*

1.1 The Four Exams This Bank Covers

1.1.1 Why one bank works for all of them

DSSSB, KVS, NVS, EMRS and most state TGT/PGT Computer Science papers draw from almost the same syllabus: computer fundamentals, digital logic, architecture, operating systems, programming, data structures, DBMS, networks, web technologies, software engineering, plus teaching methodology.

What differs is the packaging - number of questions, time, whether there is a Tier-2, and how much weight the subject section carries. The subject content overlaps heavily, which is why a single well-organised bank serves all of them.

1.1.3 What the marking scheme means for strategy

With +1 for correct and -0.25 for wrong, the arithmetic of guessing is fixed:

SituationExpected value per questionVerdict
You know the answer+1.00Attempt
You eliminated 2 of 4 options (50% chance)0.5(+1) + 0.5(-0.25) = +0.375ATTEMPT - clearly worth it
You eliminated 1 of 4 options (33% chance)0.33(+1) + 0.67(-0.25) = +0.16Attempt, marginally worth it
Pure blind guess (25% chance)0.25(+1) + 0.75(-0.25) = +0.0625Barely positive. Skip if a distractor looks deliberately attractive
You have no idea and cannot eliminate anythingEffectively 0 or negativeSKIP
TIP: The single most profitable habit

Do not aim to answer everything. Aim to eliminate two options on every question you are unsure about. That converts a coin-flip into a clearly positive-expectation attempt, and it is a trainable skill - it comes from knowing the *neighbouring* concepts, which is exactly what the explanations in this bank drill.

NOTE: Read the last three rows carefully - they decide your strategy

Row 13 (Emerging technology). The three DSSSB papers you shared barely touched AI, cloud, IoT or cyber security. But newer papers across all these bodies are adding them, and EMRS already has an explicit ICT and cyber-security component. I have therefore included a full section on it (Chapter 9.4) even though its historical DSSSB count is near zero. This is the one place where I am deliberately preparing you for a paper that may not look like the last one.

Row 15 (Physics / Accounting / Economics). This is a DSSSB peculiarity - its Discipline sections pull from the wider graduate stream. KVS and NVS Computer Science papers generally do not do this. If you are sitting only KVS or NVS, you may de-prioritise this row. If you are sitting DSSSB, you cannot - it was worth 6 to 10 marks per paper.

Row 16 (Teaching Methodology). The highest marks-per-hour-of-study in the entire syllabus, across every one of these exams. Most candidates under-prepare it because it feels less serious than Computer Science. That is exactly why it is the cheapest place to gain 15 marks.

TIP: The mistake almost everyone makes

Candidates spend 60% of their time on the topic they already enjoy - usually programming - and arrive at the exam having never once revised Teaching Methodology or MIS. Your weakest *high-weightage* topic is always worth more than your strongest one. Rank your topics by (weightage x weakness) and study the top of that list, not the top of your comfort list.

1.3 How to Use This Question Bank

1.3.1 The method

1. Pick one chapter. Cover the answers. Attempt every question, writing your choice on paper.

2. Score yourself with the real scheme: +1, -0.25, 0.

3. Read every explanation, including for questions you answered correctly. The explanations teach the *neighbouring* concepts, which is where the next paper's questions come from.

4. Keep a "leak list" - one line per question you got wrong, naming the concept, not the question.

5. After finishing all chapters, your leak list is your revision syllabus. Nothing else.

6. Re-attempt each chapter after two weeks. Anything wrong twice needs the underlying theory re-read, not just the answer memorised.

NOTE: An honest statement about sources

I have the three DSSSB papers you shared, and I have read the published syllabi of KVS, NVS and EMRS. I do not have the actual question papers of KVS, NVS or any state TGT exam.

So every question here is either marked [PYQ] - genuinely from your DSSSB papers - or [EXPECTED], meaning I constructed it from the common syllabus and the question patterns these bodies use. I have not labelled anything as being "from the KVS 2023 paper", because I cannot verify such a claim and it would be dishonest to imply it.

This matters practically too: an expected question that teaches the right concept is far more useful than a misattributed one. The concepts are what repeat across these exams, not the exact wordings.
